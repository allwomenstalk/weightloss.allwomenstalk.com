# 10 Easy Ways to Burn Extra Calories at Work \(Without Going to the Gym\)

Learning **how to burn calories at work** is one of the most effective strategies for maintaining daily fitness when your schedule is packed. Most of us struggle to find the time or energy for a full gym session after a long workday. Fortunately, making small adjustments to your daily desk routine can add up to significant energy expenditure over time. If you want to discover practical **ways to burn calories at work** without disrupting your productivity, here are 10 easy habits to build into your office day.

## 1. Knee-Lifts

There will undoubtedly be times when your job requires sitting at your desk for long, uninterrupted stretches. When that happens, seated desk exercises are a fantastic way to keep your circulation moving. Performing seated knee lifts under your desk—lifting one leg a few inches off your chair, holding for a few seconds, and alternating sides—helps engage your core and stretch tight hip flexors while burning extra energy. If you enjoy discreet desk movements, you can also try [flat belly exercises you can do while sitting](https://fitness.allwomenstalk.com/video-guide-of-flat-belly-exercises-can-do-while-sitting/) to keep your momentum going throughout the afternoon.

## 2. Build Your Abs

Another simple strategy to burn extra calories at your desk is practicing isometric abdominal contractions. Whether sitting during a video call or standing at a copy machine, simply pull your navel toward your spine, contract your abs tightly for 10 to 15 seconds, and release. Repeating this cycle for 2 to 3 minutes throughout the day helps strengthen your core muscles, improve postural stability, and boost overall caloric burn without requiring specialized equipment.

## 3. Wear High Heels

While comfortable footwear is essential for long days, wearing heels periodically can actually change your gait and muscle activation patterns. Walking in heels requires greater calf engagement and balance control, which slightly increases energy expenditure compared to wearing flat shoes. Research from [Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu) notes that subtle changes in biomechanics require muscles to work harder. Just be sure to select supportive heels with proper cushioning to avoid foot strain or blisters, and alternate with supportive flats when taking longer walks.

## 4. Walk and Talk

If your daily workload includes frequent phone calls or virtual catch-ups, turn that talking time into an active session. Investing in a wireless headset allows you to pace around your office or step outside during calls. Walking while talking can nearly double your calorie burn compared to sitting static in a chair. Additionally, standing or pacing keeps you more alert and conscious of time, helping meetings run more efficiently.

Here is a quick breakdown of average calorie expenditure per hour for common workplace movements:

| Movement Type | Approx. Calories Burned / Hour | Energy Impact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sitting at Desk | 60 - 80 kcal | Low Baseline |
| Standing at Desk | 100 - 120 kcal | Moderate (+30%) |
| Pacing during Calls | 180 - 220 kcal | High (+150%) |

## 5. Chew Gum

You might wonder: **does chewing gum burn calories**? While it sounds surprisingly simple, research published by the [National Institutes of Health](https://www.nih.gov) indicates that chewing sugar-free gum can burn approximately 11 calories per hour. Over a full workday, this low-intensity action adds up. Beyond the modest caloric uptick, chewing gum keeps your mouth active, which helps curb mindless snacking on office pastries or desk candy. Just remember to discard your gum before major meetings or client calls.

## 6. Go out of Your Way

Most of us naturally look for the shortest route from point A to point B, but taking deliberate detours is one of the easiest ways to rack up daily step counts. Whenever you step away to visit the restroom, grab a cup of tea, or consult a colleague, take the long corridor or use the stairs instead of the elevator. Taking extra steps throughout your shift increases non-exercise activity thermogenesis (NEAT), which is key to maintaining steady energy levels. For more ideas on maximizing your daily movements, check out these [exercises with major results](https://fitness.allwomenstalk.com/exercises-with-major-results/).

## 7. Drink Water

Staying properly hydrated is essential for metabolic function and energy levels. Drinking chilled water helps boost your resting metabolic rate, as your body spends energy warming the fluid to body temperature. Swapping sugary sodas or flavored lattes for plain or sparkling water eliminates hundreds of empty calories. Furthermore, keeping a large water glass at your desk ensures frequent trips to refill it, naturally encouraging you to stand up and walk around. Learn more about optimal daily hydration from the [CDC Water and Wellness Guide](https://www.cdc.gov).

## 8. Stand Often

Standing as often as possible is a powerful way to reduce prolonged sedentary time. Standing engages your legs, back, and core stabilizer muscles, burning significantly more calories per hour than sitting relaxed in an office chair. If your workspace allows, consider using an adjustable standing desk or simply standing up during routine reading tasks and phone updates. You can also cross-reference your hydration habits from [Point 7](#7) to ensure you get built-in standing breaks.

## 9. Trade Your Chair out

Swapping your traditional ergonomic chair for a stability exercise ball introduces continuous micro-movements into your workday. To remain upright on an exercise ball, your core and lower back muscles must constantly make micro-adjustments to keep you balanced. This dynamic seating style improves posture, supports spine alignment, and burns more calories than static sitting. Furthermore, you cannot roll an exercise ball across the room, forcing you to stand up whenever you need to reach for a document or supply.

Key Benefits of Stability Ball Seating:

- Engages deep core and stabilizer muscles continuously
- Promotes proper posture and spinal alignment
- Prevents slouching and reduces lower back stiffness
- Forces active standing whenever retrieving items nearby

## 10. Don't Eat Lunch at Your Desk

Eating lunch while staring at your monitor is a common habit, but it works against your fitness and mindfulness goals. Eating at your desk often leads to distracted overeating because your brain is focused on emails rather than satiety cues. Instead, step away from your desk to eat in a breakroom or take a 15-minute brisk outdoor walk before eating. Eating away from your workstation gives your mind a mental reset and keeps your body moving during the middle of the shift.

Incorporating these practical tips into your daily routine will help you stay active, energized, and toned even during long workdays. While these small adjustments may not transform your body overnight, consistently practicing these habits will raise your daily energy expenditure and support long-term health.
